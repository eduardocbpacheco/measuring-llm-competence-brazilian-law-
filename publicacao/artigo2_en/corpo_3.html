4.1Reading the tables directly

Three readings jump out of the columns. The first is that the leader depends on the task. On the discursive question and on the formal criteria of the document, the best judge is claude-opus-4-5, at 0.824 and 0.850; on the substantive criteria it is claude-opus-4-6, at 0.744. In the scores the dissociation is larger: claude-opus-4-6 takes 88.8% of the points on the discursive question and 55.5% on the formal criteria of the document, while glm-4-7, which takes 62.6% on the discursive question, reaches 66.9% on the formal part of the document.

The second is that variation across areas of law is large, and larger than the variation among judges at the top. Among the 26 measured, the distance between the best and the worst area of the same judge has a median of 0.207 on the discursive question, 0.256 on the formal criteria and 0.338 on the substantive ones. The comparison that settles the point is internal to the top: the top nine on document substance are all contained within a span of 0.047, and the range across areas within one of those same nine has a median of 0.279, almost six times larger. A judge chosen by the aggregate column may be among the best in tax law and among the worst in criminal law, and a table of one number per judge does not record that.

The third is that the formal part of the document is systematically easier to judge than the substantive part, and is the only one of the three slices where the best judges reach the human ceiling. Section 4.3 deals with this. Not every candidate reaches the tables, and the absences have distinct causes that an empty cell does not separate. Judging a document requires issuing a structured verdict on up to 38 criteria at once, and models with a 4,096-token output ceiling cannot produce that answer whole: the two Llama 3 models cover 9% and 19% of the document criteria, and are absences by structural limit. Mixtral 8x7B covers 91%, that is, it answers almost everything, and still scores a κ of 0.123, near-zero agreement. sabiazinho-4 covers 93% and omits the rest, and what it omits is not random when the omission is systematic: the 63 criteria left out in three or more of the 5 repetitions have 60.3% positive human answers against 38.0% for the rest (Fisher, p = 0.0010), that is, it stays silent preferentially on the easy criteria. Structural incapacity, capacity with null agreement and selective omission are three different situations, and all three appear as a missing score.

4.2The noise floor and the tie at the top

Running exactly the same protocol, with the same code, over the same data, does not return the same number. The median standard deviation of κ across repetitions under frozen code is 0.0101. Converted into a decision threshold at 95% confidence, two scores differing by less than about 0.04 are not distinguishable from run-to-run fluctuation. The threshold depends on the slice measured, because slices with fewer criteria fluctuate more: it is 0.040 on the discursive question, 0.035 on the aggregate document, 0.047 on the form class and 0.046 on the substance class. Applied to Table 3, the threshold ties the top nine on document substance, which are separated by 0.047 in total, and the distance between the first and the third is 0.017, less than half the threshold. gpt-oss-120b and kimi-k2-5, which this project uses in production, are in that group. It is not possible to state, with this protocol and this sample size, which of the nine is the best. The choice among them can be made on another criterion, such as cost per call, latency or availability, and that needs to be declared rather than disguised in an ordering that suggests a precision the measurement does not have.

There are two further sources of irreproducibility the protocol does not control. The first is inference batch size. The same prompt was submitted twice to the same server, at temperature zero and with a fixed seed, first alone and then inside a batch of 16 simultaneous requests: the similarity between the two outputs is 25.6%. There is no detectable bias in the mean, the difference in score between the two conditions being 1.5 percentage points at p = 0.554; what the batch prevents is sample-by-sample reproduction. The cause we estimate is floating-point arithmetic [6]: batch size changes how matrix operations are grouped on the hardware, and adding the same numbers in a different order gives a slightly different result, which at a point of near-tie between two tokens flips the choice. We tested separately whether serving multiple adapters contributed, and it does not. The second source is the identity of the model: the same identifier, called two months later through the same API with the same code, moved the document κ of gpt-oss-120b from 0.784 to 0.763. It is not possible to separate model drift, since the provider may have updated the weights behind the same name, from implementation drift.

4.3Document drafting is not a single task

The aggregate document column sums the two classes of criterion of Section 3.2, and summing them erases the difference between them. The correct comparison is paired, judge by judge, and it is emphatic: among the 26 measured, agreement on the formal part exceeds that on substance for 25, with a median difference of 0.168 (paired Wilcoxon, p < 0.0001). Judging the form of a document is a task almost every usable model does well, and the best exceed the human ceiling of 0.763 there; judging substance is where they separate.

The two orderings are also not the same. The rank correlation between the ordering by form and the one by substance is ρ = +0.48 (p = 0.015), whereas between the ordering by substance and the aggregate one it is +0.98. The aggregate document column is, for ordering purposes, the substance column: the 148 formal criteria carry too little weight to show in it, and correspond to 14.9% of the points. Choosing a judge by the aggregate column is choosing by substance without declaring that this is what is being done. If the object of measurement is whether the evaluated model gets the form of the document right, and work on domain adaptation shows that this is exactly where training moves things most [10], the ruler to consult is the form one, and it has a different leader.

4.4Judging and answering are associated, but distinct

Choosing a judge by its performance on the task itself is the cheapest practice, because it requires no human annotation. Tables 2 to 5 allow it to be tested directly, because they carry the same models in both roles over the same material. Figure 1 puts the two axes side by side, one panel per task.

Spearman's correlation is +0.67 on the discursive question (n = 29, p = 0.0001), +0.61 on the formal criteria of the document (n = 27, p = 0.0008) and +0.41 on the substantive ones (n = 27, p = 0.032). All three are positive, and the order among them is the result: the more the task demands judgment of a thesis and the less it demands conformity to a form, the less performance on the task predicts competence at judging it. It is the same axis that separates form from substance in Section 4.3, now seen from the other side. The quadrant of good judges that answer badly is populated in all three panels, and the counterexamples are strong. kimi-k2-thinking is among the best judges of documents and is last at writing them, with 15.8% of the substantive points. mistral-l3-675b is first at writing documents and fourteenth as a judge. Under selection by task performance, the two would have been ranked the other way round. Whether the association on substance is genuinely weaker than on the discursive question is undecidable at our sample size: Steiger's test for dependent correlations [9] falls 0.005 short of the conventional cut, and the confidence interval of the difference by resampling runs from −0.18 to +0.55. What holds is that the association exists, is positive, and is too weak on substance to replace calibration against humans.
